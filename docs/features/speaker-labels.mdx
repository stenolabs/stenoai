---
title: "Speaker labels"
description: "Learn how Steno labels audio channels, separates individual speakers, and optionally suggests named people across meetings."
---

Steno can show two kinds of speaker information in a transcript: audio-channel labels such as `[You]` and `[Others]`, and individual speaker groups such as `Speaker 2` and `Speaker 3`.
Named people are optional and separate from anonymous speaker separation.

## Channel labels

When Steno captures both microphone and system audio, it records them as separate channels.
The microphone channel starts with the label `[You]`, while system audio starts with `[Others]`.
This makes the two sides of a virtual meeting easy to distinguish before Steno separates individual voices within either channel.

If system audio is not enabled, the recording has one audio channel instead.
On macOS, Steno can still separate multiple people who share that microphone, including participants in an in-person meeting.
The same individual separation also applies to imported mono recordings.

See [Recording → System audio](/features/recording) for capture setup instructions.

## Individual speakers

On macOS, Steno can distinguish up to four voices within each audio channel.
The main voice on the microphone keeps `[You]`, and additional voices receive labels such as `Speaker 2` and `Speaker 3`.
The main voice on system audio keeps `[Others]`, with additional voices numbered across the meeting.

Individual speaker separation is available without creating named people or storing cross-meeting voice profiles.
Windows currently keeps `[You]` and `[Others]` channel labels but does not separate individual voices within a channel.

## Review speakers

Enable **Settings → AI → Speaker identification** before processing a meeting to make the **Speakers** review section available for that meeting.
Anonymous labels in the transcript remain available while this setting is off, but Steno does not retain the speaker data needed for review actions.
Open a processed note and review the **Speakers** section before relying on its labels.
For each speaker group, you can:

- approve or change a suggested person;
- create a new named person;
- keep the generic speaker label;
- mark a group as containing more than one person; or
- play available excerpts and compare them by ear.

Separation and name suggestions can be wrong, especially with short speech, background noise, overlapping voices, or audio leaking between channels.
Human review remains the final decision.

## Named people and voice profiles

Cross-meeting speaker identification is optional and off by default.
Existing installations are switched off once on upgrade, including installations where speaker identification was previously enabled.
You can enable it under **Settings → AI → Speaker identification**.
Anonymous `Speaker 2` and `Speaker 3` separation continues to work while this setting is off.

When you create a named person from a confirmed excerpt, Steno creates a numerical biometric voice profile on your device.
Steno uses that profile to suggest the same person in later meetings, but it never assigns the name without review.
Voice profiles and the audio used to create them are not sent to Steno or included in analytics.

Enable speaker identification only after informing affected people and confirming that you are authorised to create and use their voice profiles.
The Settings switch is the opt-in; Steno does not collect consent from the recorded person or record a separate authorization for each profile.
This opt-in does not by itself establish legal compliance for your recording or use case.

Open **Settings → People** to review known people, play a representative voice sample when its source recording and matching speaker data are still retained, or delete a profile.
Deleting a profile removes future matching for that person but does not delete recordings or transcripts, which have separate retention controls.

## Limitations

- Steno separates at most four voices per audio channel. If more people speak on one channel, additional voices can be merged into the four available speaker groups.
- A speaker can be split into multiple groups, or several people can appear in one group. Use the review actions to correct or mark these cases.
- Cross-talk, overlapping speech, background noise, and short excerpts can reduce separation and suggestion quality.
- Representative sample playback depends on a suitable source recording and matching speaker data still being available.
- Individual speaker separation and named voice profiles are currently macOS-only. Windows retains `[You]` and `[Others]` channel labels.
